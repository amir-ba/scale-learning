Plan weekly learning plan This week week 1

You learn on your own. Each week you get a handout. You plan your time, you study, you build, and you check yourself. Once a week you meet your mentor for a short sync. The sync is a checkpoint: you show what you did, and you talk about your questions. It is not a lecture.

Five rules for learning that lasts

  1. Predict, then run. Before you run a command or reload the page, think: what do I expect? Then look at what really happens. A wrong guess is good: it shows you exactly what to learn.
  2. Type, do not copy. Type the commands and the code yourself. Your hands learn too. Copy-paste only long data, never code you want to understand.
  3. Break it on purpose. When something works, change one thing and see what breaks: remove an import, rename a script, delete node_modules/. You learn more from a broken thing you repair than from a working thing you only read.
  4. Rubber duck it. After a source or a step, explain it out loud to a rubber duck (or any object on your desk) in two sentences: what is it, and why does it exist? When you must say it step by step, you find the gaps yourself (rubber duck debugging). If you cannot explain it, put it on your question list. Recall from memory is the strongest way to remember.
  5. Small steps, often committed. Use git all the time. Make a branch for each week's work. Commit each step that works. If you break something, you can go back.

Your question list

You do not need notes. Keep only one short list of the things you do not understand, e.g. a file QUESTIONS.md in your calculator repo. When you solve one yourself, tick it and add one line on the answer. Bring the list to the sync.

When you are stuck

  1. Read the error message slowly, all of it. The first line and the file name with the line number are the most important. Copy the exact error text: you need it to search and to ask.
  2. Make it smaller. Undo your last change (git diff, git stash). Does it work again? Then the problem is in that change.
  3. Search with the exact error text in quotes, plus the tool name (e.g. "Failed to resolve import" vite). Prefer results from the official docs, GitHub issues, and Stack Overflow.
  4. Ask an AI assistant to explain, not to solve. Ask "why does this error happen?" and "what concept do I miss?". Do not paste the whole task. If the AI writes code for you, you must be able to explain each line at the sync.
  5. The 30-minute rule. If you are stuck for more than 30 minutes, stop. Put the question on your list: what you want, what you did, what you saw. Continue with another part of the week. If it blocks everything, send the question to your mentor before the sync.

A good question has three parts: "I want to …", "I did … (command, code)", "I see … (exact error)". Writing it down often solves it.

What the sync is for

  • You show the "Done when" items: running code, a link to your commit or pull request.
  • You explain one or two things without notes. Your mentor picks them. This is not a test; it shows what to repeat.
  • You bring your question list.
  • Together you agree on the next week. Your mentor can change the plan if a week was too easy or too hard.

How to run the weekly sync (30–45 min)

  1. Recall (5 min). Ask 2–3 questions from older weeks without notes. Use the "Checkpoint" section of the week handout.
  2. Show (10 min). The student shows the "Done when" items. Look at the commits, not only at the result.
  3. Explain (10 min). Pick one file or concept. The student explains it line by line. Ask "why?" twice. Do not correct at once; ask a question that leads to the gap.
  4. Questions (10 min). Go through the question list. If you do not know an answer, look it up together — this shows how a professional finds answers.
  5. Next week (5 min). Hand out the next week. Agree on what to cut or extend.

After the sync, tell the teacher agent (Claude) what went well and what was hard. It writes a learning record and adapts the next week handout.

Questions about this plan? Put them on your question list and bring it to the sync. Your mentor can also ask the teacher agent (Claude) to explain a topic again in another way.
